# Design

## Context

See proposal.md (Why). The animation system from `kalman-animations` works like this: `samplePose()` blends two keyframe poses with one easing, and every layer uses the same keyframes at the same moment. A layer can only turn (`rotate`, limited by the rig's range) and move (`offset`). The rig is generated by `npm run cut:kalman` from the settings in `scripts/cut-kalman-layers.ts`, and `check:kalman` fails when the generated files differ from what the script writes. The same script already cuts **drawn views** for the turntable (a whole drawing per angle and mood, placed on the front figure's ground line), and the figure cross-fades between them (`kalman-turntable.ts`, `kalman-figure-client.ts`).

Facts the numbers below rely on: the front body's pivot is at its feet (y 951 of 962), so it squashes towards the ground; the front head is 278 units tall; the side figure's bill points left, its body may turn ±100° and its feet ±10°. On the side body a negative rotation tips the head towards the bill (forward): −90° is horizontal with the bill forward.

Raymond's choices (PLANNING_LOG, 2026-10-27): a deeper bow on the **front** figure, **squash and stretch** in this change, swim and launch as full parts of it, and the bowed images used as a **whole drawn view**, regenerated with warmer eyes.

| **Dive** | A little goofy, but natural; he enters the water face first. | A line of penguins at an ice edge, each in another phase: waddling up, leaning far forward with the flippers back for balance, a small hop, then head first into the water, the body almost horizontal and pointing down. |

| **Launch** | A very professional launch, but a goofy landing, as if he almost loses his balance. | Rising vertically like a rocket with the flippers pressed to the body; bursting out of the water at an angle with the feet trailing; landing on the feet, some tipping onto the belly and sliding. |

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Every effect is a pure function of time, so scrubbing, the snapshot tests and the unit tests keep working exactly as now: the pose at 0.73 s is always the same.
- Tools that work for every animation (lag, overshoot, stretch, drawn poses), not one-off code for the bow or the dive.
- Raymond tunes the numbers by eye in the preview; the values in this design are starting points.

**Non-Goals:**
- Physics or springs that depend on the previous frame (see decision 2).
- Changing the idle loop's keyframes.
- Water, ice, splashes or fish: the scene is Phase 2. The fish he glances at is not drawn; only his glance is.
- Bending a layer (a curved tail): a layer stays one rigid image that may turn, move and stretch.
- More drawn poses than the bow; the mechanism allows them later.

## Decisions

### 1. Stretch is one number per layer that keeps the area
A pose gives a layer `stretch: s`: the layer is drawn `s` times as tall and `1/s` times as wide, in its own upright direction, around its pivot (0.9 squashed, 1.1 stretched, 1 unchanged). The local transform becomes *move to position + offset → turn around the pivot → stretch around the pivot*, so the stretch turns with the layer.

- **Why one number:** squash and stretch keeps the volume, so a squashed ball gets wider. One number does that automatically and needs one slider per layer.
- **Alternative:** a separate `[sx, sy]` scale: twice the sliders, and easy to shrink him by accident.

### 2. Lag is a time shift per layer, not a spring
An animation may give `lag: { "tail": 0.12 }` in seconds. When sampled at time `t`, a layer with lag `L` takes its values from the pose at `t − L`; the others use `t`. Before the start, a non-looping animation holds its first pose; in a loop, `t − L` wraps around the loop's length.

- **Why:** still a pure function of time (scrubbing works, tests are exact), and one small loop in `samplePose()`.
- **Alternative:** a spring per layer (like head-follow's `gsap.quickTo`): good when playing, but a scrubbed moment would differ from the played one and snapshots would be flaky.

### 3. Length = last keyframe + largest lag (non-looping only)
A non-looping animation lasts until its slowest layer has caught up: `playLength = last keyframe + max(lag)`. The player's tween, the scrub slider's range and "finished" all use it. A loop keeps its keyframe length, because its lagging layers wrap.

### 4. Overshoot: `back.out` and `back.inOut`, with clamping
The two `back` eases are allowed: they go slightly past the target and settle back. To keep the promise that no layer turns or stretches past its range, the sampled pose is **clamped** to the rig by a pure `clampToRig(pose, rig)` in `kalman-rig.ts`, applied by the player before drawing. Offsets have no range and aren't clamped.

- **Alternative:** extra keyframes for every overshoot: doubles the keyframes and the timing is fiddly to tune.

### 5. Children inherit the stretch through the matrix chain
`resolvePose()` already passes each layer's movement from rest to its children. With the stretch in the local matrix, children get it too: when the body squashes towards its feet, the head, flippers and tie move down with it and stay attached, and squash a little themselves, as a cartoon squash does.

- **Alternative:** children follow only the moved attachment point and keep their shape: a second movement per layer, and the tie would no longer fit the squashed belly.

### 6. Stretch and rotation ranges in the rig, from the cut script
`RigLayer` gets an optional `stretch: [min, max]` that must include 1; a layer without it may not stretch. The ranges are settings in `scripts/cut-kalman-layers.ts`, so `rig.json` stays fully generated. Starting ranges: the front and side **body** [0.85, 1.15], the **head** [0.92, 1.08]. The side **body** rotation becomes [−150, 100], so he can go into the water head down. The side **head** may turn up to 70° back (was ±30°): when he lies horizontally the bill would otherwise point at the floor, and with the head turned back it points forward, bill first, as in Raymond's underwater stills. *(Found in the swim's film strip during apply.)* The side **feet** become ±20° so they can trail, and share **one pivot**, so they always turn together as one piece. *(Changed during apply, approved by Raymond: at ±30° and with two pivots the cut showed. The feet are now cut from their dark pixels only, with a soft ankle like the neck, and the body keeps a fading strip under the ankles, so a turning foot takes no white feathers along and leaves no hole.)*

### 7. The drawn bow is a whole drawn pose, cut like a turntable view
Raymond's bowed images redraw the whole upper body (hunched shoulders, head sunk into the chest), while the lower half lines up exactly with `front-goofy.png`. So the bow uses them as a **whole drawing**, not as a new head layer. The cut script cuts the figure out of the white background and places it exactly like the front figure (same scale, same spot), with its colours untouched: as edits of `front-goofy.png` the lower half already lines up pixel for pixel, so the feet stand on the front figure's ground line and the start of the cross-fade matches the layers. *(Refined during apply: the design first said "like a turntable view", with its plumage matching and re-centring, which would only have moved a drawing that already lines up.)* It writes `src/scene/kalman/drawn-poses.json` with a goofy and a professional image per drawn pose. `KalmanFigure.astro` draws them hidden, like the turntable views.

- **Why:** it reuses the cutting, placing and mood handling the turntable already has, and only the upper half differs, so a cross-fade looks smooth.
- **Alternative:** a head-only image variant per layer. That needs images in which only the head changes, which Gemini didn't produce, and a new variant mechanism in the rig.

### 8. A keyframe can name a drawn pose; the fade is part of sampling
A keyframe may say `"drawn": "bow"`. The layered pose of that keyframe (`bowDeep`) still blends as usual underneath. On top, the drawing's opacity is a pure function of time (`drawnFrame()`, next to `samplePose()` rather than inside its result, which keeps both simpler): 1 from that keyframe until the next keyframe without `drawn`, and it ramps from 0 to 1 over the **fade** time (0.2 s, tunable) before the drawn span and back down over the fade time after it. The player sets it on the drawing, and the layers fade the opposite way. *(Changed during apply: not as `o` and `1 − o`, which made him see-through halfway, as the bow's film strip showed. `crossFade()` fades the drawing in over the solid layers first and then the layers out under the solid drawing.)*

- **Why:** a cross-fade, not a switch, so nothing pops; and as a function of time, the scrub slider and snapshots see exactly what playing shows.
- **Alternative:** switching at the keyframe: simpler, but a visible jump.

### 9. Tempo and travel (decided by Claude at Raymond's request)
- **Tempo:** an animation may be longer where the feeling needs it: bow ~2.4 s (a polite moment at the bottom), dive ~2.2 s, swim loop ~3.6 s (three strokes and a glance), launch ~2.8 s (with the wobble). Tests read lengths from the data.
- **Travel:** he stays in his spot sideways; he moves up and down only where the story asks for it (the hop, going down into the water, the launch and the landing). Swimming stays in place: in Phase 2 the scene moves past him, and sideways travel would fight that layout.

At `bowDeep`, the head offset of 80 is more than a quarter of the head's height (278), as the spec asks, and it brings the layered head close to where the drawn bow has it, so the cross-fade has little to bridge.

### 11. Eyes that follow (added after the first round)
The eyes are painted into the head (goofy) and into the glasses layer (the calm professional eyes), so the pupils can't move yet. Like the side flipper, a moving part needs painted ground underneath: Raymond makes two edits **without pupils**, `front-goofy-no-pupils.png` and `front-professional-no-pupils.png`.

- **Cutting:** the head comes from the goofy edit without pupils, and the glasses layer (glasses and calm eyes) from the professional one. The **goofy pupils** are where `front-goofy.png` differs from its edit, the **professional pupils** where `front-professional.png` differs from its edit, each as one layer for both eyes (they move together). Both hang from the head, so they turn with it, and are drawn above the head and the glasses.
- **Mood:** the professional pupils are accessories (professional only); the goofy pupils get a new field `mood: "goofy"` (shown only in that mood), so they never peek out behind the calm eyes.
- **Limit:** a layer may get `shift: [x, y]`, the furthest its offset may go from rest each way; validation rejects more, and `clampToRig()` clamps to it, so a pupil stays inside its eye. Starting value: about a third of an eye's width sideways and a quarter up or down, measured on the images.
- **Following:** a pure `eyeShift(point, eyes, shift, reach)` next to `headTurn()`: the offset grows with how far the point is from the eyes, in x and y, up to `shift`. The player glides the pupils there with `gsap.quickTo`, faster than the head (0.15 s against 0.3 s), so the eyes lead and the head follows: overlap again.
- **Alternative:** drawing round pupils in code would be simpler, but Kálmán is drawn from Raymond's art only.

### 12. Preview
Each layer with a stretch range gets a stretch slider (step 0.01) below the rotation sliders, built from the same template. `preview-pose.ts` merges stretches like rotations, and Copy pose leaves out stretches at 1, as it leaves out rotations at 0. The scrub slider's range becomes `playLength`. Drawn poses are not posable; they appear only through an animation.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The regenerated bow images no longer line up in the lower half] → The same alignment check as for the side images (best shift 0, 0 against `front-goofy.png`) before cutting; if they don't line up, Raymond regenerates them with "change nothing below the shoulders".
- [The cross-fade shows a ghost of the head in two places] → `bowDeep` puts the layered head close to the drawn one, and the fade is short (0.2 s, tunable); the snapshot "halfway through the cross-fade" makes it visible in review.
- [The drawn bow's colours differ from the layers] → The cut script matches its plumage tone to the front view, as it does for the turntable.
- [Stretching a child inside a squashed parent shears it slightly] → The ranges stay small (±15%); checked by eye and with the snapshots.
- [Squash opens a seam at the neck or shoulders] → The head and flippers move with the body through the matrix chain, and the neck fade already hides the head's edge; the ranges can shrink if a seam shows.
- [Wider side ranges (body −150°, feet ±20°) show a cut edge] → Checked at the ends of the range before the poses use them; a data test keeps both side feet turning by the same amount.
- [Lag makes a layer look detached] → Only its own rotation and stretch lag; where it hangs is still decided by its parent *now*.
- [Clamping flattens an overshoot at the end of a range] → Only visible when a pose sits at the edge; such poses get a little room, or the ease becomes `power2.out`.
- [The horizontal swim reads as lying still] → The surge, the drift and the trailing feet carry the motion; in Phase 2 the scene moving past him adds the speed.
- [Longer animations change the scrub range and the e2e expectations] → The tests read the range from the data.
- [New snapshot baselines] → Made with "Update visual baselines" and checked by eye, as before.

## Migration Plan

No migration: `rig.json` is regenerated by `npm run cut:kalman`, `drawn-poses.json` is new, and existing poses without `stretch` and animations without `lag` or `drawn` mean exactly what they meant before. The reference stills stay out of the repo: a `references/` line in `.gitignore` lets Raymond keep them next to the project without committing other people's footage. Rollback is reverting the branch.

# Design

## Context

See proposal.md (Why). The animation system from `kalman-animations` works like this: `samplePose()` blends two keyframe poses with one easing, and every layer uses the same keyframes at the same moment. A layer can only turn (`rotate`, limited by the rig's range) and move (`offset`). The rig is generated by `npm run cut:kalman` from the settings in `scripts/cut-kalman-layers.ts`, and `check:kalman` fails when `rig.json` differs from what the script writes. The front body's pivot is at its feet (y 951 of 962), so the body turns and would squash towards the ground. The front head is 278 units tall. The side feet may turn only ±10°.

Raymond chose a deeper bow on the **front** figure, and squash and stretch **in this change** (PLANNING_LOG, 2026-10-27).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Every effect is a pure function of time, so scrubbing, the snapshot tests and the unit tests keep working exactly as now: the pose at 0.73 s is always the same.
- Tools that work for every animation (lag, overshoot, stretch), not one-off code for the bow or the dive.
- Raymond tunes the numbers by eye in the preview; the values in this design are starting points.

**Non-Goals:**
- Physics or springs that depend on the previous frame (see decision 2).
- Changing the idle loop's keyframes.
- Motion blur, smear frames or new illustrations.
- Bending a layer (a curved tail): a layer stays one rigid image that may turn, move and stretch.

## Decisions

### 1. Stretch is one number per layer that keeps the area
A pose gives a layer `stretch: s`: the layer is drawn `s` times as tall and `1/s` times as wide, in its own upright direction, around its pivot. So 0.9 is squashed, 1.1 stretched, and 1 is unchanged. In matrix terms the local transform becomes *move to position + offset → turn around the pivot → stretch around the pivot*, so the stretch turns with the layer.

- **Why one number:** squash and stretch in animation keeps the volume: a squashed ball gets wider. One number keeps that automatically and needs one slider per layer.
- **Alternative:** a separate `[sx, sy]` scale. More freedom, but twice the sliders, and it makes it easy to shrink him by accident.

### 2. Lag is a time shift per layer, not a spring
An animation may give `lag: { "tail": 0.12, "footFront": 0.1 }` in seconds. When sampled at time `t`, a layer with lag `L` takes its values from the pose at `t − L`. The other layers use `t`. This is follow-through and overlap: the body moves first and the tail follows a moment later. Before the start, a non-looping animation holds its first pose. In a loop, `t − L` wraps around the loop's length, so the tail carries the end of one stroke into the start of the next.

- **Why:** it is still a pure function of time (scrubbing works, tests are exact), and it is one small loop in `samplePose()`.
- **Alternative:** a spring per layer (for example `gsap.quickTo`, as head-follow uses). It looks good when playing, but its value depends on the previous frames, so a scrubbed moment would differ from the played one and snapshots would be flaky.

### 3. Length = last keyframe + largest lag (non-looping only)
A non-looping animation now lasts until its slowest layer has caught up: `playLength = last keyframe + max(lag)`. The player's tween, the scrub slider's range and "finished" all use `playLength`. A loop keeps its keyframe length, because its lagging layers wrap.

### 4. Overshoot: `back.out` and `back.inOut`, with clamping
The two `back` eases are added to the allowed eases: they go slightly past the target and settle back, like a head that nods a bit too far and then corrects. To keep the spec's promise that no layer turns or stretches past its range, the sampled pose is **clamped** to the rig: a pure `clampToRig(pose, rig)` in `kalman-rig.ts` that the player applies before drawing. Offsets have no range, so they aren't clamped.

- **Alternative:** extra keyframes for every overshoot. That works without new eases, but it doubles the keyframes and the timing is fiddly to tune.

### 5. Children inherit the stretch through the matrix chain
`resolvePose()` already passes each layer's movement from rest to its children. With the stretch in the local matrix, children get it too: when the body squashes towards its feet, the head, flippers and tie move down with it and stay attached. They also squash a little themselves, which is what a cartoon squash looks like.

- **Alternative:** children follow only the moved attachment point and keep their shape. That needs a second movement per layer, and the tie would no longer fit the squashed belly.

### 6. Stretch ranges in the rig, from the cut script
`RigLayer` gets an optional `stretch: [min, max]` that must include 1. A layer without it may not stretch, and validation says so. The ranges are settings in `scripts/cut-kalman-layers.ts`, so `rig.json` stays fully generated. Starting ranges: the front and side **body** [0.85, 1.15], the **head** [0.92, 1.08]. The side feet's rotation range grows from ±10° to ±30°, so they can trail in the dive.

The bow's deepest head offset of 80 is more than a quarter of the head's height (278), as the spec asks.

### 8. Preview
Each layer with a stretch range gets a stretch slider (step 0.01) below the rotation sliders, built from the same template. `preview-pose.ts` merges stretches like rotations, and Copy pose leaves out stretches at 1, as it leaves out rotations at 0. The scrub slider's range becomes `playLength`.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Stretching a child that turns inside a squashed parent shears it slightly] → The ranges stay small (±15%), so the shear is a few pixels; checked by eye in the preview and with the "squashed body" snapshot.
- [Squash opens a seam at the neck or shoulders] → The head and flippers move with the body through the matrix chain, and the neck fade already hides the head's edge; a snapshot of the squashed bow covers it, and the ranges can shrink if a seam shows.
- [Wider side-foot range shows the foot's cut edge] → Checked at ±30° in the preview before it goes into the dive; the range is lowered if an edge shows.
- [Lag makes a layer look detached] → Only its own rotation and stretch lag; where it hangs is still decided by its parent *now*, so it can't come loose.
- [Clamping flattens an overshoot at the end of a range] → It is only visible when a pose sits at the very edge of the range; such poses get a little room, or the ease becomes `power2.out`.
- [Longer non-looping animations change the scrub range and the e2e expectations] → The tests read the range from the data (`playLength`), not a fixed number.
- [New snapshot baselines] → Made with "Update visual baselines" and checked by eye, as before.

## Migration Plan

No migration: `rig.json` is regenerated by `npm run cut:kalman`, and existing poses without `stretch` and animations without `lag` mean exactly what they meant before. Rollback is reverting the branch.
